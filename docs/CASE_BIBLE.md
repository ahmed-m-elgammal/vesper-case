## 0. Premise and fairness rules

- **Victim**: Edmund Vesper, 62, celebrated author-illustrator of the "Hollow" children's books (Whistler the Fox). Widower. Failing heart, kept steady by Dr. Helena Marsh's tincture of digitalis (five drops in water).
- **Where**: 7 Hollow Lane — a three-storey brick townhouse (ground: entrance hall, parlour, kitchen, study; upper: two bedrooms and a bathroom; attic studio; basement cellar and laundry/cot).
- **When**: Saturday the 14th of October, one stormy night. Power cut by lightning at 10:47 pm, restored at 11:21 pm.
- **Cause of death (true)**: digitalis (foxglove) overdose slipped into the **whiskey decanter** in the parlour; Edmund's nightly nightcap ritual did the rest. Certified (falsely) as heart failure by Dr. Marsh herself.
- **Fair-play contract**:
  - The minute-by-minute truth (§1) is written first; every clue in `clues.json` must be consistent with it.
  - No required clue is hidden behind a pixel hunt; every gated item has a tool or a statement that names it.
  - Every red herring resolves (§7). No dead-end clues without a purpose.
  - The culprit, method and motive are all deducible from what the player can find, room by room.

## 2. The crime, mechanically

- **Agent**: crushed foxglove leaf (digitalis purpurea) — grows in Dr. Marsh's own medicinal garden bed; also wild on the lane (so *source alone must not convict* — preparation and presence do).
- **Onset**: ingested ~11:24, first symptoms 11:38, death ~11:40. Dose calibrated by someone who understands digitalis (a physician) — but the player proves *presence + preparation*, not pharmacology alone.
- **Destruction**: stems/petals burned in the parlour hearth; pages 40–43 of the memoir taken from the house; her umbrella never entered the hall stand; she came and left by the garden door.
- **Her mistakes** (the evidence chain):
  1. Two purple petals + a leaf midrib survive in the hearth ash.
  2. A dull film (varnish ring) above the liquor line in the glass — and the same film on the decanter's inner shoulder: the *decanter* was dosed, not the glass.
  3. The garden door bolt left off, fresh scratches on the latch plate.
  4. A diamond-tread boot print, size 7–8, rain-damp on the rug — matching nobody's footwear in the hall.
  5. The torn slip tucked at page 39: *"for H.M. — before Monday. E.V."*
  6. No doctor's coat or umbrella in the hall — yet Dr. Marsh's name sits in Colette's ledger for Friday's call, and her certification at 7:20 am was *very* quick.

## 3. Suspect sheets

### 3.1 Margaret "Peggy" Vesper-Croft — 38 — the niece
- **Public face**: moved in eight months ago "to keep house for uncle"; expects the house; drowning in IOUs and a final lending-office notice.
- **Statement**: "In my room from half past eight with a novel and my sewing. Asleep before eleven — I heard him wind the hall clock before bed, as he always does."
- **Truth**: she came down at 10:40 for her scissors, traded a few cheerful words with Edmund, went back up, slept. The Tuesday letter terrified her — but she had no need to kill before Tuesday.
- **Lie**: "heard him wind the hall clock" — a clock that had been stopped since the blackout. Disproof: hall clock (10:47) + her candle burned to the eleven-o'clock mark (Act 3).
- **Red herring**: debts + inheritance motive; her scissors in the parlour (innocently left at 10:40 — resolves via timeline entry).
- **Evidence that clears her**: scissors timeline; her candle mark; the will letter's actual content (Act 3 — "instructions for the trust", not money).

### 3.2 Julian Ashcombe — 45 — the literary agent
- **Public face**: visitor at nine, gone by a quarter past ten.
- **Statement**: "I left at ten sharp. We spoke of sales and nothing else — I never heard of any memoir, and I never saw one."
- **Truth**: left at 10:15 (ledger); guilty of **forgery** (selling Tobias's copies as originals), not murder. He begged in pencil — the impression survives on the study blotter (Act 3).
- **Lie**: "never heard of any memoir." Disproof: blotter impression + cab receipt 10:19 (he was gone before the murder window — his alibi actually clears him once the times are fixed).
- **Red herring**: the forgery guilt reads as motive; his dry umbrella and the doormat prove he never returned.

### 3.3 Dr. Helena Marsh — 51 — the family physician — **THE CULPRIT**
- **Public face**: keeper of Edmund's failing heart; certified the death at 7:20 am; trusted by everyone.
- **Statement**: "I was at my surgery all evening. He rang at half ten about his drops; I told him to come Monday. I never left the house I was in."
- **Truth**: Ruth Marsh — her elder sister — was Edmund's true studio partner, dead since 1949 (a fall on the attic stair on a storm-lit night). Edmund kept Ruth's surviving sketchbooks and built the Hollow books on them. His memoir was his deathbed attempt to put it right. Helena came at his summons (10:56, garden door), read pages 40–43, begged him to burn them, was refused — and dosed the decanter at 11:15. Her motive (see §5 twist): the confession, as Edmund intended it, would have told Ruth's last night *wrong* — and the only person left who knew the truth of that night was Edmund himself.
- **Lie**: "never left the surgery." Disproof: tread prints (size 7, diamond), garden bolt off at 11:30, no coat/umbrella in the hall while her ledger name says she attends regularly, hay-and-honey smell of burned foxglove, her fast certification the next morning.
- **Red herring**: her prescription label on the pill bottle (Act 1) — the poison's easiest source, the cheapest misdirection. The bottle is nearly neat: the drops were not the vehicle.
- **Conviction stack** (Act 5): presence (bolt + prints + ledger) · preparation (physician, medicinal bed, calibrated onset) · opportunity (alone with Edmund 10:58–11:17; the decanter) · the slip (H.M.) · her statement collapsing under three separate proofs.

### 3.4 Tobias Grimm — 29 — the live-in apprentice
- **Public face**: inking in the attic from half seven to midnight; cot in the basement laundry.
- **Statement**: as above, plus "a clatter like a clock falling, a few minutes after half eleven."
- **Truth**: exactly what he says. His half-finished sketch is the *timeline's truth-teller* — the pencil line stops where the power died (10:47) and resumes after 11:21. He ghost-drew the last two books; his copies were the forgeries Julian sold.
- **Secret** (Act 4/5): the ghost-drawing; his copies aged with tea.
- **Red herring**: the horn button on the stair carpet — his, lost days before, swept along. The button resolves in Act 3 when a spare matches it in his sewing tin.

### 3.5 Colette Beaumont — 34 — the housekeeper
- **Public face**: kitchen from half six; baked through the blackout; found the body at 7:05.
- **Statement**: as above, plus "I found the garden door off the bolt at half eleven and bolted it — thought I'd left it so."
- **Truth**: exactly what she says. Her ledger is the most honest witness in the house.
- **Secret** (Act 4): she has been skimming the housekeeping tin for her sister's debts and hid the ledger page — shame keeps her stories tidy, and makes her a suspect briefly.
- **Red herring**: the burnt loaf (blackout casualty; the pantry lantern proves her movements).

## 5. The 60% twist (Act 4)

The player expects the memoir to *redeem* Ruth (Edmund's confession naming her the true artist). The attic shows the truth: Ruth's sketchbooks, dated years before "Edmund created" Whistler — and on the last page, her pencil note: *"H. carried the light away — R."* The landing candle stub, the sticking stair door bolted from outside "as a joke": **Ruth's death was not the accident Edmund believed — Helena took the light that night and never came back for her.** Edmund's memoir (pp. 40–43, found as carbons in the study safe, Act 3) would have enshrined his own guiltless version forever. Helena killed Edmund believing she was protecting Ruth's memory; the twist is that the confession would have protected it better. Every Act-1 clue reads differently after this: the doctor's quick certification, the hay-and-honey ash, the pages only she carried out.

## 8. Content pipeline notes

- All case facts live in `public/content/*.json` (validated at boot by `src/game/data/validate.ts`; machine-readable schemas in `public/content/schemas/`).
- Suspect `secret`/`lie`/`redHerring` fields ship in the data from day one but are surfaced only by Act 5 systems.
- New acts = new rooms in `rooms.json`, clues in `clues.json`, gates in `gates.json`, dialogue nodes in `dialogue.json` — **no engine changes required**. This is the core benefit of the data-driven MVVM split: content authors never touch TypeScript.
